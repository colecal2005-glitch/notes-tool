Why I stopped optimizing my mornings

For two years I tracked every morning habit: cold shower, journaling, no phone for 60 minutes, a 20-minute walk, then 90 minutes of deep work before email. I had a spreadsheet. I was proud of it.

Then I had a bad month. Client left, freelance dried up, I got sick for two weeks. The routine fell apart completely.

Here's what I noticed when it came back: the habits I rebuilt weren't the ones on my spreadsheet. I dropped the cold shower immediately. I stopped timing my deep work blocks. I still journal, but only when I want to, not on a schedule.

The insight I couldn't see while I was optimizing: I had confused the feeling of being productive with actually producing anything. The spreadsheet gave me that feeling at 7am every day. So did the cold shower. So did the no-phone rule. None of it correlated with output. Some of my best writing happened after sleeping in, skipping the walk, and opening email first thing.

The morning routine industrial complex sells certainty. Wake up at 5am and your life will be controlled. Track your habits and you'll feel like a person who has their act together. What it doesn't tell you: the feeling is the product, not the output.

I still have a morning routine. It's: make coffee, read something I didn't write, start working. That's it. Takes about 20 minutes. On the days I do this, I write more than I did during the two-year optimization era.

The thing I was actually optimizing for wasn't performance. It was the anxiety of not knowing if I was doing enough. The routine was a security blanket dressed up as productivity.
